# Kulas family notes (Polish line, Kryzsko side)

The Kulas family are cousins of Patrick Kryzsko on the Kryzsko side. Spelling variants to
search: **Kryzsko**, **Krzysko**, **Kulas**.

**How they connect** [Geni GEDCOM export (26 Jul 2026)]: Patrick's
grandfather Alfred James Krzysko (1907–1995) had a
sister, **Clara Maria Krzysko** (1905–2002), who married **Ludwig Joseph Marion "Louis" Kulas**
(1892–1976). Their sons (the authors of sources 1–3; living, so details are in the private repository)
are first cousins of Patrick's father, Francis Duane Kryzsko (1935–2017). **Harry Kulas** was a son of Louis's
brother **Anthony F. Kulas** (1872–1952), so Harry was James's first cousin, as source 2 says.
He was related to Patrick by marriage, not by blood.

All facts below come from family-written histories (secondary sources). Check them against
records before promoting this file to `source`.

## Harry Kulas (1900–1963)

- **Born:** 3 October 1900, Warsaw, North Dakota (per his military records) [1][2]
- **Parents:** Anton (Anthony) Kulas and Frances (Prondzinski) Kulas. Harry was their first
  child [1][2]. His military records give his mother as Frances L. Lamb, so she probably
  remarried [1][2].
- **Siblings:** one brother and two sisters (sisters unnamed in these sources) [2]
  - **Cyril E. Kulas**, the second child, married Jean Gorman and lived in San Francisco and
    Santa Clara, California. He may have been a German POW in World War II [1].
- **School:** Williston High School, ND; right halfback on the 1917 North Dakota state
  championship football team (beat Devils Lake 51–20 on 23 Nov 1917). Graduated 1919 [1][2].
- **Work:** about 8 years as a heavy-construction carpenter (railroad bridges, water towers,
  buildings) before enlisting [1][2].
- **Military:** active duty 22 Nov 1927 to 30 Nov 1947, retiring at Camp Stoneman,
  California. Acting First Sergeant, 228th Signal Operations Company, Philippines,
  12 Sep 1941 – 9 Apr 1942 [1][2].
  - Captured 9 April 1942. Survived the Bataan Death March and Camp O'Donnell, where he
    nearly died of dysentery and was nursed back by men of his company [1][2][3].
  - Shipped to Japan in August 1944 on the *Noto Maru*; held at Camp Omori, Tokyo, as a
    forced laborer. Recovered at Yokohama on 6 September 1945 [1][2].
- **Marriage/children:** none known [1][2].
- **Died:** 23 December 1963, Letterman General Hospital, San Francisco (acute myocardial
  infarction) [1][2].
- **Buried:** 31 December 1963, Golden Gate National Cemetery, San Bruno, San Mateo County,
  California, plot Z 2298 [1][2].
- **Headstone:** "Harry Kulas, Minnesota, M SGT, US Air Force, World War II, October 3 1900 –
  December 23 1963" [4]. The narrative says Army. Both may be right (the Air Force became
  separate from the Army in 1947, the year he retired), but this needs checking, as does the
  "Minnesota" on the stone.
