# Murphy–Sherlock marriage and Rev. Fr. Dominic Lavin (Irish line)

Line: Patrick → Francis Duane Kryzsko (1935–2017) → Margaret Evelyn Murphy (1915–1986) →
James Patrick Murphy (1883–1969, born in North Dakota) → **James Patrick Murphy** (15 Apr 1854 –
22 Apr 1942), Patrick's great-great-grandfather → Arthur Murphy (1822–1891) → Patt Murphy.

## The marriage

- **James Patrick Murphy** married **Elizabeth Anne Sherlock** (12 Jul 1856 – 30 Jan 1920) on
  **2 May 1876** at **St. Peter Celestine Catholic Church**, Pakenham, Lanark County, Ontario. The marriage was performed by
  **Rev. Fr. D. J. (Dominic) Lavin** [1].
- Lanark County, Ontario marriage register entry, dated 3 Jun 1876 (probably the civil
  registration date) [2].
- Elizabeth's parents were J. Patrick Sherlock (about 1832 – before 1862) and Mary Devine (1834–1898) [3].

## Register entry (transcription)

St. Peter Celestine, Pakenham, parish register, entry M.1 of 1876, signed by D. J. Lavin. The image
came from FamilySearch via Patrick. A copy is kept privately in Genealogy-Living at
`data/visual/ireland/murphy-sherlock-marriage-1876-pakenham.png`.

> St. Celestine, Pakenham, the second day of May, one thousand eight hundred and seventy-six.
> Whereas a dispensation of two of the banns of marriage has been granted by the undersigned, in
> virtue of a power accorded by His Lordship, Dr. Duhamel, Bishop of Ottawa; whereas also the
> publication of the third bann has been made at the prone of the parish mass between James Murphy,
> laborer, son of age (23 years) of Arthur Murphy & Mary Thorp [?] of this mission, on the one part;
> and Anne Sherlock, daughter of age (20 years) of Patrick Sherlock (deceased) and Mary Devine of
> this mission, on the other part. No impediment having been discovered, I the undersigned priest
> of this mission, having received their mutual consent to marriage, have given them the nuptial
> benediction on the day and date above mentioned, in presence of Charles McGuire & Bridget Sherlock,
> who have not signed. — D. J. Lavin, Priest

Margin: "Murphy James and Sherlock Mary [?]", marked "Registered".

Notes:
- James's mother is given as **Mary Thorp**. She is Mary Thorpe in the GEDCOM; see below.
- The bride is called **Anne**, matching Elizabeth Anne Sherlock. The margin seems to say "Mary".
- The witness **Bridget Sherlock** is probably the bride's sister, Bridget Ellen Sherlock (born 1858).
- Ages: James 23 and Anne 20. That fits Anne's birth in 1856, but for James (born 15 Apr 1854,
  so 22 in May 1876) it is about a year off.

## Arthur Murphy and Mary Thorpe (James's parents)

- **Arthur Murphy Sr.** (12 Mar 1822 – Mar 1891), farmer, was born at **Enniscorthy, County Wexford**.
  His parents were Patt Murphy and Catharine Pettit. He was buried in Grand Forks, ND. His Geni
  sources include County Wexford census material.
  Find a Grave: https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/21723405/arthur-murphy
- **Mary Thorpe** (Nov 1817 – 23 Feb 1909, East Grand Forks, MN; buried in Grand Forks). The GEDCOM
  gives her baptism as **16 Nov 1817 at St. Catherine's, Meath Street, Dublin**, with a baptism
  image on Geni. Find a Grave: https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/21723420
- Patrick's family knowledge is that both came from **County Wexford**. Mary's Dublin baptism doesn't
  necessarily contradict that: she may have been baptized in Dublin and raised in Wexford, or two
  Mary Thorpes may have been mixed up. **Open question.**
- Their children (GEDCOM): James Patrick (1854), Margaret (1857), Garrett G. (1859), Mary Anne (1861),
  Philip (1863) and Arthur (1864–1885). In 1876 the family was in the Pakenham mission, Ontario.
  Arthur and Mary later farmed in Ferry Township (Grand Forks County, ND) until 1891.

## Ferry family connection

- **Margaret Murphy** (11 Jun 1881 – 1968), daughter of James Patrick Murphy and Elizabeth Anne
  Sherlock (so Patrick's great-great-aunt), married **Joseph Stewart Ferry** (1878–1954), son of
  Peter Ferry and Julia A. Hickey (GEDCOM). Their children were first cousins of Margaret Evelyn Murphy.
- **Ferry Township** (Grand Forks County, ND), where Arthur and Mary Murphy farmed, surrounds the city of
  **Manvel** (founded 1881) (Wikipedia, "Ferry Township, North Dakota"). Whether the township was
  named after this Ferry family is unconfirmed.

## Rev. Fr. Dominic Lavin

- Not a blood relative. Patrick found him through the marriage register and created his Geni
  profile, linking the stories about him: https://www.geni.com/people/Rev-Fr-Dominic-Lavin/6000000087720895149
- **Dominic Joseph Lavin**, born 3 Aug 1842 at Riverstown (Riverston), County Sligo, Ireland. His
  parents were Thomas Lavin and Honora (Hanora) McGowan [4][5].
- **Famine passage:** the family emigrated in "Black '47". Cholera killed his father and an uncle,
  who were buried at sea, and his two brothers, who died near Quebec City, probably at Grosse Île.
  Only Dominic and his mother survived. His mother placed him in the care of Archbishop Bourget of
  Montreal and Fr. Anthony O'Malley [4].
- He studied at St. Sulpice Seminary, Montreal (1861) and was ordained in Montreal in 1865. After a
  short time at Allumette Island, he became pastor of Pakenham in November 1866 [4].
- He was pastor of St. Peter Celestine, Pakenham (and Fitzroy Harbour), for 37 years. He was known
  for his care of the poor, for arranging adoptions of orphans and for building the present church.
  His mother lived in his rectory [4][5].
- He died at Pakenham on 12 Dec 1903, aged 61, of stomach cancer. The Requiem Mass was sung by his
  cousin Rev. J. McGowan of Fort Covington, NY. His will left $6,000 for two seminary scholarships
  at the University of Ottawa [5].
- He is buried beside his mother (died 6 Apr 1879, aged 63) in St. Peter Celestine RC Cemetery
  ("Indian Hill"), Pakenham. The headstone reads "Rev. Dominic Joseph Lavin, Pastor of Pakenham" [5].
- He married James Patrick Murphy and Elizabeth Anne Sherlock on 2 May 1876, ten years into his pastorate.
